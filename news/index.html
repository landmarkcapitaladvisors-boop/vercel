Infrastructure and economic development

Why It Matters

Why infrastructure matters

Infrastructure development can have an impact far beyond the individual project being built. Better roads, transport links, industrial corridors, utilities and connectivity can encourage businesses to establish operations in new areas — and support residential and commercial development around them.

The link to real estate

Infrastructure and real estate often develop alongside one another. A new road or transport connection can improve accessibility to a previously less-connected area; industrial development can create employment, which in turn increases demand for housing and supporting commercial services.

For investors, understanding these connections can be just as important as looking at the individual property or project.

Landmark Capital Advisors and the AIF market

Alternative Investment Funds

Alternative Investment Funds (AIFs) provide structures that differ from traditional mutual funds and publicly traded products. India's AIF market has expanded steadily, with investors looking at specialised opportunities across real estate, private companies, infrastructure and credit — putting growing attention on fund managers and the strategies they use.

Key sectors to watch

Four Areas, Ranked by Coverage Depth
01

Warehousing

India's logistics requirements are increasing as businesses expand distribution networks. Demand is shifting toward facilities with good road connectivity, efficient layouts, appropriate storage capacity and proximity to major consumption or manufacturing centres — making Grade-A warehousing an increasingly important part of India's commercial real estate market.

02

Logistics

Logistics moves products from manufacturers to distributors, retailers and customers. Companies are looking at ways to reduce delivery times and improve efficiency, encouraging the development of strategically located logistics facilities across major markets — with knock-on effects for commercial real estate and infrastructure demand.

03

Infrastructure

Infrastructure remains one of the major drivers of India's long-term economic development. Improved infrastructure makes it easier for businesses to transport goods, access markets and establish operations in developing regions — a key consideration when assessing the potential of a location or asset.

04

Residential Real Estate

Housing demand stays closely connected with India's growing cities and changing demographics. As cities expand, demand can move beyond established central locations into developing suburbs and emerging urban corridors, shaped by roads, transit, employment centres, schools and healthcare access.
